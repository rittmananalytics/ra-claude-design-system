Approach · read carefully

The delivery approach. It has changed.

The SOW's Appendix [X] described a projection-based remodelling on top of the existing gold layer. That approach has been superseded by what was agreed in the onsite planning session on 13 May. The current approach is:

Step 01 · Phase 1RA

Lift and shift.

Copy all Snowflake data to BigQuery exactly as-is. No transformation, no remodelling. RA handles Fivetran repointing, BQ Transfer Service for bulk historical, dbt syntax conversion. Gold layer lands in BigQuery in the same shape.

Step 02 · Phase 1Steph / Carwow

Looker on as-is data.

Steph puts Looker directly on the lifted BigQuery data. Hits the August Tableau deadline without RA building a projection layer. RA supports and advises; this Looker implementation is primarily Steph's work.

Step 03 · Discovery sprintRA

New gold layer design.

RA designs the proper new gold layer from first principles. Genuine ground-up entity modelling — conceptual, logical, physical specs. Begins in this discovery sprint.

Step 04 · Phase 2 (separate SOW)RA

New gold layer build.

Build and test the new entity-based gold layer in BigQuery. Progressively migrate Looker explores. Properly address entity join problems (incl. GYC). 12–24 month programme — this is what Carwow actually bought when they chose us over a lift-and-shift vendor.

Implication for this sprint: the workshops are not projection-scoping sessions. They are genuine design sessions for the new gold layer. Appendix [X]'s exclusions about "end-user or engineer-led remodelling workshops" were written for the old approach and are not operative constraints on what we design in discovery.

Workshops · framing

What the domain workshops are (and are not).

Workshops are design sessions for the new gold layer. The question in every one: what are the canonical entities in this domain, how do they relate, and what are the authoritative KPI definitions? Participants are domain experts — Steph, leads, analysts who built the models — not end-user stakeholders articulating dashboard wishes.

In scope

  • Canonical entity definitions — what is an Order, User, Vehicle, Dealer, Lead in this domain
  • Existing join keys — what actually joins in source data vs what is worked around with aggregation
  • Conformed dimensions: User, Vehicle, Dealer, Date, Country, Channel
  • KPI definitions — the single authoritative definition of each metric this domain owns
  • Entity resolution problems — document explicitly as Phase 2 design inputs; do not attempt to solve in this sprint

Not in scope

  • "What dashboards do you want?" — that's Phase 2 requirements work
  • Ingestion or pipeline design
  • Anything requiring remodelling of source data or transformation logic
  • Agreeing new joins that have no basis in the existing source data

The tone that will not work: facilitated wishlist sessions. Steph has flagged this explicitly. If a session drifts toward "what would you like to see in reports," redirect to "what does this entity represent and how does it connect to that one."

The hard design question

The GetYourCar problem. Know this before you walk in.

The most important single design question for the sprint. Raise it explicitly in the Tuesday GYC sessions.

The problem

A customer can submit a GYC enquiry without completing a configuration. Configurations and enquiries do not have a 1:1 join relationship. No reliable common join key exists in source data.

Why it matters

The metric the entire business wants is configuration-to-enquiry conversion rate. Currently impossible at transaction level. Workaround is an aggregated table (dealership × product × day) which avoids the join but is use-case-specific.

What Phase 1 does

Nothing. The aggregated table lifts as-is to BigQuery. Steph's Looker sits on it in its current form.

What discovery must produce

  • A clear written articulation of the entity relationship problem — what it is, why it exists
  • Investigation of whether a join key could be derived from source data — session IDs, event timestamps, probabilistic matching
  • A proposed entity model for Phase 2 — even if Phase 2 requires ingestion changes to implement, document what right looks like
Do not leave the Tuesday sessions without surfacing and documenting this. Ask directly:

"How do you currently compute configuration-to-enquiry conversion rate, and what table does that come from?"
